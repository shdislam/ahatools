🎂 Age Calculator

Enter your date of birth to get your exact age.

About this tool

This age calculator works the same way official offices do — full calendar years first, then leftover months, then days. People use it for birthday planning, school/admission forms, visa and job applications where you must state age in exact years, months and days.

How to use it

  1. Pick your date of birth in the box above.
  2. Press Calculate Age — no clicking through dropdowns needed.
  3. Want total days lived instead? Use our Days Between Dates tool with your birth date as the start.

FAQ

How is age calculated exactly?

It counts complete calendar years between your birth date and today, then remaining whole months, then remaining days — borrowing days from the previous month when needed (e.g. from Aug 30 to Aug 24 next year gives 11 months and 25 days).

Why do some sites show a different age?

Sites that divide total days by 365.25 get slightly wrong month/day values. Calendar-accurate counting like this one matches what appears on official documents.

⚖️ BMI Calculator

Body Mass Index using centimeters and kilograms.

About this tool

BMI (Body Mass Index) is the screening measure recommended by the World Health Organization to check whether your weight is in a healthy range for your height. It takes seconds, needs only a tape-measure-free height value in centimetres and weight in kilograms.

How to use it

  1. Enter height in cm (e.g. 170) and weight in kg (e.g. 65).
  2. Your BMI shows instantly with its WHO category: under 18.5 underweight · 18.5–24.9 normal · 25–29.9 overweight · 30+ obese.
  3. The result also shows the healthy weight range for your exact height.

FAQ

What is the BMI formula?

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²). For example 65 kg at 1.70 m → 65 ÷ 2.89 = 22.5.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not perfectly — muscle weighs more than fat, so very muscular people can score "overweight" while being healthy. Treat BMI as a quick screening number, not a medical diagnosis; for body composition ask a professional.

🏦 Loan EMI Calculator

See your exact monthly payment before you borrow.

About this tool

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is the fixed amount you pay a bank every month on a loan. This calculator shows that payment before you sign anything — plus the total interest you'll pay over the full duration, which banks don't highlight upfront.

How to use it

  1. Enter loan amount, annual interest rate and duration in years.
  2. Check the monthly EMI — compare it against your monthly income (most advisors say keep it under 40%).
  3. Look at Total interest: try shortening the duration and watch how much money it saves.

FAQ

What is the EMI formula?

EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P = principal, r = monthly rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = months. It's the standard reducing-balance formula used by banks worldwide.

How can I pay less interest overall?

Two proven levers: shorter tenure (biggest saving) and prepayments early in the loan, when most of each EMI is still interest. Compare scenarios above before committing.

🏷️ Discount Calculator

Know the real price before you pay.

About this tool

Sale tags are designed to confuse. This discount calculator tells you the real final price and exact savings instantly — useful during sales seasons, market bargaining, or checking whether a "70% OFF!" deal is actually good.

How to use it

  1. Type the original price and the discount percentage.
  2. Read what you'll actually pay at checkout.
  3. Comparing two offers? Run both here instead of doing mental math.

FAQ

What's the formula?

Final price = price × (1 − discount ÷ 100). On 2,000 with 25% off: 2000 × 0.75 = 1,500 saved 500.

Is 25% + 10% off equal to 35% off?

No — stores apply them one after another: 25% then 10% of the reduced price equals only 32.5% total. Chain them through this tool twice to see real stacked prices, and use our Percentage Calculator for other comparisons.

％ Percentage Calculator

Three most-used percentage calculations.

About this tool

Percentages trip everyone up because there are three different everyday questions — "what's X% of Y", "X is what percent of Y" and "how much did it change". This page answers all three live as you type, no button needed.

The three calculations

  1. X% of Y — tips, taxes, commissions. Example: 10% of 200 = 20.
  2. X is what % of Y — test scores, market share. Example: 45 of 200 = 22.5%.
  3. % change — price rises, growth reports. Example: 80 → 100 = +25% increase.

FAQ

What is the percentage change formula?

((new − old) ÷ |old|) × 100. Going from 80 to 100 is (20 ÷ 80) × 100 = 25% up; the reverse is −20%, because percentages aren't symmetric.

Where does this help daily?

Restaurant bills, exam marks, salary hike offers, discount stacking (see our Discount Calculator) and loan interest comparisons.

🎓 GPA Calculator

Pick a grade and credits for each course.

About this tool

This GPA calculator computes your credit-weighted grade point average on the standard 4.00 scale used by most universities in the US, Bangladesh, Pakistan and beyond. Credits matter: an A in a 4-credit course moves your GPA more than an A in a 1-credit lab.

How to use it

  1. Pick a letter grade and credit hours for each course.
  2. Add more rows for extra courses with one click.
  3. Your GPA appears instantly weighted by credits.

FAQ

What's the formula?

GPA = Σ(grade points × credits) ÷ Σ(credits). Grade points: A=4.00, A−=3.70, B+=3.30, B=3.00, C+=2.30, C=2.00, D=1.00, F=0.

Is GPA the same as CGPA?

GPA covers one semester; CGPA averages all semesters so far. Calculate each semester here, then average them credit-weighted for CGPA. Some universities use different scales — check yours before applying scholarships.

🔤 Case Converter

Fix text capitalization in one click.

About this tool

Fixing ALL-CAPS text someone sent you, formatting headlines, or cleaning up data entries — this converter switches between UPPERCASE, lowercase, Title Case and Sentence Case in one click, then copies the fixed version straight to your clipboard.

The four modes

  1. UPPERCASE / lowercase — for codes, headings, or calming down shouty emails.
  2. Title Case — capitalizes Each Word, ideal for video titles and product names.
  3. Sentence case — capitalizes the first letter of each sentence only; best for body paragraphs.

FAQ

When should I use Title Case vs Sentence case?

Title Case for titles, headlines and YouTube video names; Sentence case for descriptions and paragraphs — it's easier to read in long form.

Does it work with any language?

Fully for English text. For mixed Bengali/English content the English parts convert correctly while other scripts pass through unchanged.

🖼️ Image Compressor

Your photo never leaves your device — everything happens in your browser.

About this tool

Photos from phones are often 3–8 MB — too big for forms, email limits and slow websites. This compressor shrinks them to a fraction of the size, and because everything happens on your device via HTML Canvas, your pictures are never uploaded anywhere.

How to use it

  1. Choose an image (JPG or PNG).
  2. Pick quality: 60–75% is the sweet spot — visually identical for photos at half the size.
  3. Compress & Download — very large images are also resized down to max 1600px, perfect for web use.

FAQ

Which quality should I choose?

70% suits most photos. Go 85–95% for prints or text-heavy images; below 50% you'll start seeing blur around sharp edges.

Why did my PNG get bigger/smaller than expected?

Output is JPEG, which compresses photographs extremely well but flattens transparency. For logos with transparent backgrounds, keep the original PNG instead.

About this tool

Weak passwords are behind most account takeovers. This generator uses your browser's cryptographic random number generator (Web Crypto API) — the same standard used by security software — and creates passwords that never leave your device because there's no server to send them to.

How to make a strong password here

  1. Keep length at 16+ characters — length beats complexity every time.
  2. Leave all character sets checked for maximum strength.
  3. Generate → Copy → paste into the site's password field. Save it in a password manager.

FAQ

Is this password stored anywhere?

No. It exists only in your browser tab's memory until you close it. There's no logging, no server, no database — verify by going offline: it still works identically.

Can hackers guess crypto-random passwords?

Not practically. A 16-character random password has ~10²⁵ combinations; at a trillion guesses per second that's over 80 million years of average search time.

📏 Unit Converter

Convert length and weight instantly.

About this tool

Converts length (m, km, cm, mm, mile, foot, inch, yard) and weight (kg, g, lb, oz, tonne) using internationally exact factors — not rounded approximations — so results match engineering and shipping requirements.

How to use it

  1. Pick Length or Weight.
  2. Choose the from/to units and enter a value.
  3. Result appears with up to 6 decimal places of precision.

FAQ

What conversion factors are used?

The exact international ones: 1 inch = 0.0254 m, 1 mile = 1609.344 m, 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 oz = 0.028349523 kg — same values defined by NIST standards.

Why do other sites show slightly different numbers?

They round the factors first (like 1 lb ≈ 0.454), which introduces error that grows with big values. This tool converts from exact base units instead.
